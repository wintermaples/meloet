# M6 Three more built-in effects: gate, de-esser, saturation

## Architecture

- One file each in `crates/meloet-sound/src/effects/`: `gate.rs`, `de_esser.rs`, `saturation.rs`; `create` in `mod.rs` makes them. The stub `pass.rs` is gone. All three keep the promises of `meloet_engine::Effect` like the first six: `latency()` is 0 (no look-ahead, no oversampling), nothing allocated or locked in `process` / `set_param` / `reset`, parameters move over 20 ms (`dsp::Ramp`, `SMOOTH_S`), a block that ends with NaN or infinity clears the state and is silent, filter states and envelopes under 1e-18 become 0 (`dsp::flush`; no FTZ/DAZ).
- New shared part in `dsp.rs`: `Svf` / `SvfCoefs`, one channel of Simper's linear trapezoidal state variable filter (the same design as the EQ's bands; the EQ keeps its own copy). `Svf::tick` gives band and low, `Svf::high` the high-pass, `Svf::flush` is called once per block.
- Play and export make effects in one place (`meloet-core/src/mixbook.rs`, `effects::create`), and the three effects have no clock or random part, so the export is the same samples as playing.
- **No read-back.** The spec section asks for no meter of the reduction for these three. The gate keeps its gain in `Gate::gain` and the de-esser its cut in `DeEsser::cut_db`; if a meter is wanted later, read those through the mechanism M2 builds for the limiter (do not build a second one).

### Gate (`gate.rs`)

- Both sides share one gain; the level is the larger of `|left|` and `|right|` of the frame (no envelope filter, so no delay in seeing a start).
- State: `hold` (frames the gate still stays open; 0 = closed) and `gain`.
  - A frame above the threshold, or — while open — above the closing level (threshold − 3 dB, `HYSTERESIS_DB`), sets `hold` to 30 ms (`HOLD_MS`). Any other frame counts `hold` down. So the gate closes 30 ms after the **last** frame above the closing level; a sine passing through zero does not close it, and it never closes sooner than 30 ms after opening.
  - Open: the gain rises in a straight line, 0 → 1 in 1 ms (`OPEN_MS`), and stops at exactly 1.0. Closed: the gain is multiplied down at 60 dB per release time (`RELEASE_FALL_DB`) and stops at the range's gain.
- The threshold and the range ramp (as sample value and as gain) over 20 ms; the release time applies at once (it is a rate, not a level).
- A new or reset gate starts closed (gain = the range).
- **Neutral**: with range 0 dB the output is the input bit for bit, always. With any range, once open the gain is exactly 1.0, so loud sound passes bit for bit (tested).

### De-esser (`de_esser.rs`)

- Watching side: the input through a 24 dB/oct Butterworth high-pass at the frequency (two `Svf` sections, 1/Q = 1.8478 and 0.7654), then a peak follower (instant up, falls to 37 % in 5 ms) so the level is the peaks and not the wave; the larger of the two sides. 24 dB/oct was chosen so the body of a voice (2–4 kHz) does not set it off at a frequency of 6.5 kHz.
- The cut wanted is `clamp(level − threshold, 0, reduction)` dB (what is over, at most the reduction; hard knee). The cut follows it with 1 ms up and 50 ms down (`ATTACK_MS`, `RELEASE_MS`; fixed).
- Working side: one high shelf (the EQ's shelf, Q 0.707) at the frequency, down by the cut, on both sides alike. So the band above the frequency goes down and what is below stays: at the frequency itself the shelf is half way (in dB), an octave and a half above it is all the way. The shelf's coefficients are computed every frame while the cut is not 0 (one `exp`, one division).
- A shelf instead of "split in two bands, lower one, add": a split with a 12 dB/oct high-pass added back as `x − (1 − g)·high` makes a bump of more than 1 dB under the frequency; the shelf has none, and when the cut is 0 the code passes the input through untouched (the filter states still run, so the shelf starts from the sound when it next moves).
- **Neutral**: reduction 0 dB, or nothing over the threshold above the frequency: the output is the input bit for bit (tested with noise and with a 500 Hz sine at defaults). After a cut, the gain is back to exactly 1 within about a second.

### Saturation (`saturation.rs`)

- The curve: `y = R · tanh(z·x / R) / tanh(z)`, with `R = REFERENCE = 0.125` (−18 dBFS) and the bend `z ≥ 0`. Whatever `z`, a sample at `R` stays at `R` — this is the "levelling inside". Quiet sound is lifted by `z / tanh(z)`; **the drive parameter is that lift in dB** (`bend_for` solves `z = lift · tanh(z)` with Newton's method in f64, at most 40 steps, in `set_param`; no allocation). At drive 0 the bend is 0 and the curve is a straight line; the code then skips the curve.
- Why −18 dBFS: the built-in sounds peak near −12 dBFS for one key and sit at −17 to −24 dB RMS. With `R` at −12 dBFS their loudness rose by up to 12 dB at full drive; with −18 dBFS it stays within about −4 … +6 dB at any drive (table under Measurements). Louder material than that gets quieter as the drive goes up; the output parameter is there for it. One constant to change if the owner's ear says otherwise.
- `tanh` is `curve()`: Lambert's continued fraction cut after seven terms (a ratio of two polynomials), flat from ±4.97; within 1e-4 of `tanh`, never above 1, no libm call in the loop.
- After the curve: the tone, a 12 dB/oct Butterworth low-pass (`Svf`). From 18 kHz up it fades out and at the maximum (20000 Hz) it is off (`TONE_OPEN_FROM_HZ`, `TONE_OPEN_AT_HZ`); the filter still runs so it has the sound when it comes back in. Then `out = (dry + mix·(wet − dry)) · output`.
- The bend, the tone (in log frequency), the mix and the output gain ramp over 20 ms.
- No oversampling (it would add delay), so a high note at a high drive aliases; the tone parameter takes some of it off.
- **Neutral**: drive 0 dB, tone 20000 Hz, output 0 dB (any mix), or mix 0 % with output 0 dB (any drive and tone): the output is the input bit for bit (tested, at five sample rates).

### Traps

- A sine at a quarter of the sample rate (12 kHz at 48 kHz) can have its frames miss the tops by up to 3 dB; a test that checks "what is over" must not use it.
- A click meter that looks at the steepest step is blind for a jump that falls where the wave is steepest; the de-esser's test looks at the difference to the untouched low part instead.
- `curve()` must be flat a hair before the place where the fraction reaches 1 (4.97, not 4.9718), or f32 rounding gives 1.0000001.

## Spec rules

- Gate 「ゲート」 (`gate`): しきい値 −80.0–0.0 dB (−45.0), 下げる量 −60.0–0.0 dB (−60.0), 戻る速さ 5–1000 ms (120). Opens in 1 ms, stays open 30 ms after the sound last was above the closing level, closes 3 dB under where it opens (all fixed). 戻る速さ is the time the sound takes to fall 60 dB. Left and right move together. 下げる量 0 dB does nothing.
- De-esser 「ディエッサー」 (`deEsser`): 周波数 3000–10000 Hz (6500), しきい値 −60.0–0.0 dB (−30.0), 下げる量 0.0–18.0 dB (8.0). Watches only the band above 周波数; what is over しきい値 is taken off the band above 周波数, at most 下げる量. Left and right move together. Nothing over the threshold: the sound is untouched.
- Saturation 「サチュレーション」 (`saturation`): ひずみ 0.0–36.0 dB (6.0), 明るさ 1000–20000 Hz (20000), まぜる量 0–100 % (100), 出る大きさ −24.0–+6.0 dB (0.0). ひずみ 0 dB is no distortion at all; 明るさ 20000 Hz is the filter off.
- None of the three delays the sound.
- Two factory presets each (table above). So every built-in effect has presets now, and the M1 note "a heading with no rows still shows (gate, de-esser, saturation have none yet)" no longer happens.
- No new key, no new step name.

## Readings

- **Gate, 戻る速さ**: the spec gives only "5–1000 ms". Read as the time to fall 60 dB (like the reverb's decay), at a steady rate in dB; with a smaller 下げる量 the gate reaches its floor sooner (−24 dB after 40 % of the time).
- **Gate, "開いたあと 30 ms は閉じない"**: the 30 ms count from the last frame above the closing level, not only from the opening; else a low note would close the gate at every pass through zero.
- **Gate, 開くのは 1 ms**: a straight line of the gain from 0 to 1 in 1 ms (from −60 dB it takes the same 1 ms).
- **De-esser, "帯に分けて足し戻す"**: built as a moving high shelf, not as two bands added back (reason under Architecture). It keeps the rule the sentence is there for, and more: with nothing to do, the output is the input exactly.
- **De-esser, 周波数**: the shelf's middle. A sound right at the frequency gets half the cut (in dB); the full cut is reached about an octave and a half above. With the defaults a 7 kHz tone at −10 dB goes down 4.6 dB, not 8.
- **De-esser**: attack 1 ms and release 50 ms are fixed (the spec names no times); left and right are linked (the spec says so only for the gate).
- **Saturation, ひずみ**: the lift of quiet sound in dB, with the curve bending around −18 dBFS. This is not "gain before tanh at full scale": at ひずみ 6 dB a sound at −6 dBFS is already bent strongly and comes out lower. ひずみ 0 dB is exactly clean, which is what the spec's test asks ("ひずみ 0 でまぜる量 100 のとき大きさが変わらない"): here it is unchanged bit for bit.
- **Saturation, 明るさ 20000 Hz = off**, fading in between 18 and 20 kHz, so the default does not touch the sound.
- **Saturation, 出る大きさ** is the last step, after the mix (it also turns the dry part).
- Preset ids and names were made up here (`builder:2026-11-24`, in `ui/src/text/ja.ts`): `preset.gate.noiseOff` 「雑音を消す」, `preset.gate.shortTail` 「余韻を短く切る」, `preset.deEsser.softS` 「サ行をやわらかく」, `preset.deEsser.firmS` 「サ行をしっかりおさえる」, `preset.saturation.warm` 「あたたかく」, `preset.saturation.rough` 「荒くひずませる」. The spec says only "M6 の3つにも2つずつ".
- Value texts follow the existing rule of `decimals()` (dB one place, Hz and ms none), so 「−45.0 dB」「120 ms」「6500 Hz」; `paramRows.ts` needed no change.
- The scene `presetsNone` keeps its name; it is now "a gate whose values are not a preset's" (its list has the two factory rows).
- The AI link: the text of the `effect` argument of the insert tool names the nine built-in effects (`crates/meloet-ai/src/tools/mixer.rs`). No new tool.

## Measurements

`cargo test --release -p meloet-sound --test effects_speed -- --ignored --nocapture` (prints all nine, steady and with every parameter moving), and `--test mixer_speed` for the whole mixer.

Numbers from the tests (48 kHz, not speed):

- Click ratio (steepest step around a jump over the steady sound; limit 2.5): gate threshold 0 → −80 dB (opening) 1.47; every other parameter of the three ≤ 1.00.

- De-esser cut: 12 kHz with frequency 3000 Hz: −3.00 / −7.99 / −17.95 dB for 3 / 8 / 18 dB; 4 dB over the threshold: −3.91 dB; at the frequency with 12 dB: −6.00 dB; 300 Hz while 9 kHz is cut by 11 dB: 0.000 dB. 7 kHz at defaults: −4.63 dB at 48 kHz, −4.64 at 44.1, −4.57 at 88.2 and 96, −4.56 at 192.

## Left for later

- **Nothing was listened to.** The three effects, the reference level of the saturation and the six presets were judged by numbers only. Listening files: `MELOET_EAR_DIR=<folder> cargo test -p meloet-sound --test effects_ear -- --ignored --nocapture` writes ten WAV files (48 kHz, 16 bit): `gate-dry`, `gate-wet` (threshold −38, range −60, release 120), `gate-wet-range-12` (range −12, release 300) — the built-in electro kit with hiss at −50 dB and a 100 Hz hum under it; `deesser-dry`, `deesser-wet-default`, `deesser-wet-strong` (5000 Hz, −40 dB, 18 dB) — **not a voice**: a 140 Hz buzz through three resonances with bursts of bright noise as "s"; `saturation-dry`, `saturation-wet-default`, `saturation-wet-warm` (drive 12, tone 6000), `saturation-wet-hard` (drive 30) — the built-in bass and pad. The de-esser should be tried on a real voice.
- Not tried in the real app (only tests and pictures of fake scenes). `tools/measure/export_app.py same` was not run with the new effects.
- The saturation aliases at high drive on high notes (no oversampling, by the no-delay rule). A way without delay (for example a minimum-phase filter pair around a 2× curve) could be looked at later.
- The gate reads single frames; a sound whose tops are more than 30 ms apart (under 17 Hz) would open and close.
- The gate and the de-esser show no meter of what they take off (not in the spec).
- The EQ still has its own copy of the filter that `dsp::Svf` now also holds; they could share it.
